### Engagement

**You own getting existing users more of the value they came for. Not more of their time.**

1. Name the core action: the thing that, done once more, means the user got more value. Opens and time spent are not it.
2. Describe the distribution of that action per user per period: median, p90, and the share who did it zero times. Report the distribution, never the mean alone.
3. Split users into the lifecycle states (`references/lifecycle.md`). How many engaged, at risk, power?
4. Study the power users: what do they use that others do not? Which of those features have most users never opened?
5. For each unused valuable feature, decide: is it undiscovered, or unwanted? Discovery is a design problem. Unwanted is a product problem.
6. Look at the at-risk group: what did they do less of in the period before they slipped?
7. Check every idea against `references/principles.md`, "What this stack will not help build". An idea that raises the number without raising the value is rejected here.
8. Pick one change, with the core action as the metric and retention as the guardrail (**experiment**).

**Reply:** the core action and its distribution, the lifecycle split, what power users do differently, undiscovered against unwanted, the at-risk pattern, and the one change with its guardrail.
